Phase 0: Empirical Spectral Profiling

Gate G0 — Energy ratio between lowest and highest band ≥2× for ≥2 graph families
DECISION: GO — 9/9 families pass (ratios 3.8×–17.0×)

Method

For each graph family we computed the normalized Laplacian eigendecomposition, partitioned eigenvalues into B = 8 uniform-width bands on [0, λmax], projected node features onto eigenvectors per band, and measured squared-coefficient energy. We fit power-law and exponential decay models and averaged over 3 random seeds for synthetic graphs.

Node features for synthetic graphs were generated by low-pass filtering random signals through exp(−tL) to produce realistic smooth graph signals.

Key Findings

1. Non-uniformity is universal and strong. Every tested graph family shows energy ratios well above the 2× threshold. The effect is strongest for BA(m=2) at 17× and weakest for SBM(q=0.01) at 3.8×. This conclusively supports the premise that graph signal energy is non-uniformly distributed across spectral bands.
2. Graphs do NOT follow a simple 1/f decay. Unlike images (where FANS exploits a ~1/f spectral decay), graph spectral energy profiles show complex, family-specific patterns. SBM energy concentrates in low-to-mid bands with peaks shifting as modularity increases. BA(m=2) shows exponential decay consistent with tree-like structure, while higher-m variants are flatter. Citation networks show moderate power-law decay with energy spread across all bands. A blanket inverse-power weighting scheme is suboptimal — weighting must be data-driven and family-specific.
3. Low R² values indicate complex structure. Decay model fits have low R² (0.05–0.51), confirming spectral energy is not well-described by simple parametric decay. Profiles are hump-shaped, bimodal, or irregularly distributed. This argues for per-family empirical profiling rather than assumed functional forms.

Implications for Graph-FANS

  1. Importance profiling must be empirical, computed per training distribution
  2. The inverse-power weighting from FANS should be replaced with direct importance weighting from measured band energies
  3. Band count B=8 captures sufficient spectral resolution
  4. Different graph families need different noise schedules
